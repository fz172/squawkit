WHY SKIPPERS USE IT

Boat maintenance that keeps the season easy

The season starts easy when the last one ended right. Impeller yearly, before the vanes give out. Lower-unit oil, checked for milk before you refill. Anodes replaced at half gone. Bottom paint and through-hulls while she is out. Safety gear checked against the flare dates. Winterizing before the first freeze. Oil and filter at 100 engine hours or twelve months, the hours counted off the propulsion itself. Add what is yours: battery conditioning over the winter, a bilge pump test each spring. Then turn the key on the first warm Saturday and go.

STARTER SCHEDULE

The season, already laid out

Add a boat and the recurring marine jobs are there waiting. Keep them, move them to the months your season actually runs, or turn off what does not apply.

Service Comes due Why
Engine oil & filter 100 hrs or 12 months Whichever comes first. Check the manual for the interval that applies to your engine.
Raw-water pump impeller 12 months Rubber vanes fail without warning and take the engine’s cooling with them.
Lower unit / gear oil 12 months Milky oil means a failed seal — check the drained oil before refilling.
Winterize 12 months Before the first freeze: drain or antifreeze the raw-water side, fog the engine, stabilize the fuel.
Sacrificial anodes 12 months Replace at half gone. Sooner in a marina with stray current.
Bottom paint & hull inspection 12 months Antifouling, through-hulls, and a look at the running gear while it is out.
Safety gear check 12 months Fire extinguisher gauge, flare expiry dates, life jacket condition.
Registration renewal 12 months Annual in most places; some states register for two or three years.

Recommendations, not authority — your engine manual and your water win. Anything else is a service you add with your own interval: battery conditioning over the winter, a bilge pump test each spring, a rig check before a long passage.

HOURS

Hours that belong to the engine, not the hull

Engine Hours

The meter is attached to the propulsion part, so on a twin the hours are each engine’s own and a 100-hour service counts the right ones. You record the reading on the service record; the dashboard keeps the current figure and the number of records beside it.

Seasons and hours together

Marine work is mostly calendar — winterize, haul out, paint — with the engine jobs on hours. A service recurs on either, or on whichever comes first, and due-soon reminders arrive before the freeze rather than after it.

SETTING ONE UP

What it asks for, and nothing it does not

The boat

Make, model, year and hull ID. That is the whole identity block — a centre console and a forty-foot sloop take the same four fields.

What hangs off it

Propulsion, electrical and safety, steering and rigging. Propulsion and the electrical and safety gear repeat, so twin engines, two batteries and the bilge pumps each get their own entry with their own serials.

THE PEOPLE WHO HELP

The yard, the co-owner, the next owner

Invite them to one boat

Sharing is per boat. Invite a mechanic or a co-owner: they see that boat’s schedule, issues and history and can log work against it, while you stay the owner and can end access at any time. SquawkIt records an ABYC certification number against a mechanic who holds one.

At the dock, out of signal

The iPhone and Android apps keep your records on the device and sync when you reconnect, so the checklist works where the bars do not. Attachment files need a connection to open, and the web app needs one throughout. Export any boat as a ZIP — PDF, CSV and spreadsheet, attachments included.

QUESTIONS

Boat questions

Something else? Contact support.

Does it work for outboards and inboards?

Both. Propulsion is a repeatable part, so a single outboard, twin outboards, or an inboard with a shaft and running gear all fit the same record. Engine Hours belong to the propulsion part rather than the hull.

Can it remind me to winterize?

Winterizing is in the starter schedule as an annual service, with due-soon reminders before the date. Move it to the month your first freeze usually lands and it comes round on that schedule every year.

Does it work at the dock without a signal?

The iPhone and Android apps keep your records on the device and sync when you reconnect, so you can log work and check the schedule out of signal. Attachment files need a connection to open, and the web app needs one throughout.

Can I share the record with my yard?

Yes. Invite a mechanic to that specific boat and they can see its schedule and history and log work against it. SquawkIt records an ABYC certification number against a mechanic who holds one.
