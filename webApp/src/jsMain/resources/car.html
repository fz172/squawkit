WHY DRIVERS AND RIDERS USE IT

Longevity and value preservation

Running like new is a schedule kept. Oil and filter at 5,000 miles or 6 months, whichever comes first. Tires rotated every 6,000 so all four wear evenly. Engine and cabin air filters yearly. Brake fluid at 24 months, before it absorbs water and loses its boiling point. Coolant at 60. Registration each year. Add your own — a detailing season, the fluid you prefer — and every record carries the odometer and the receipt. Years of that and the history becomes the asset: export the vehicle, photos included, and the care is visible to anyone who asks.

STARTER SCHEDULE

The maintenance schedule, already filled in

Add a vehicle and these come with it. Keep them, change the intervals to match your manual, or turn off the ones that do not apply.

Service Comes due Why
Engine oil & filter 5,000 mi or 6 months Whichever comes first. Check the manual for the oil grade and the interval that applies to your engine.
Tire rotation 6,000 mi Evens out wear across all four tires. Cars and trucks only.
Engine air filter 12 months Sooner on dusty roads; a clogged filter costs power and fuel.
Cabin air filter 12 months Keeps the ventilation clean. Cars and trucks only.
Brake fluid flush 24 months Brake fluid absorbs water over time and loses its boiling point.
Coolant flush 60 months Long-life coolants run further — confirm the interval in the manual.
Registration renewal 12 months Annual in most places; adjust to your state or province.
Chain lube & adjustmentOff by default 500 mi Motorcycles with a chain final drive. Sooner after rain.

Recommendations, not authority — your owner’s manual wins every time, and every interval here is editable. Anything it does not cover is a service you add: a detailing season, a track-day check, the fluid you prefer.

ODOMETER

Miles or months, whichever comes first

One meter that matters

The odometer. You log it as you go, and every service record keeps the reading it was taken at — so the history is mileage-stamped rather than a list of dates. The dashboard shows the current reading and how many records the vehicle has.

Reminders before it lapses

A service recurs on miles, on months, or on whichever lands first — the way the manual actually writes it. Due-soon and overdue reminders arrive before the date, and anything that puts the vehicle off the road sits at the top of the list.

SETTING ONE UP

What it asks for, and nothing it does not

The vehicle

Make, model, year and VIN. Nothing else is required — a bike with no VIN plate and a 1972 project car both fit.

What hangs off it

The engine, and the tires as a set: each one with its position — front left through spare — and its normal PSI, so the pressure you are meant to run is recorded where you will look for it.

THE PEOPLE WHO HELP

Your shop, your partner, your history

Invite them to one vehicle

Sharing is per vehicle. Invite a mechanic or whoever else drives it: they see that vehicle’s schedule, issues and history and can log work against it. You stay the owner and can end access at any time. SquawkIt records an ASE certification number against a mechanic who has one.

The history is the asset

Export the vehicle and you get a ZIP: the full record as a PDF, a CSV and a spreadsheet, with every receipt and photo you attached. A documented, mileage-stamped history is the difference between “it was looked after” and showing that it was.

QUESTIONS

Vehicle questions

Something else? Contact support.

Does it work for motorcycles as well as cars?

Yes — one vehicle type covers both. The starter schedule includes chain lube and adjustment for chain final drive, and marks the services that are cars and trucks only, so you keep what applies and turn off what does not.

Can a service come due on miles instead of a date?

Yes. A service recurs on the odometer, on calendar time, or on whichever comes first — an oil change at 5,000 miles or 6 months behaves the way the manual describes it.

Can I keep more than one vehicle?

Yes. Every vehicle is its own record with its own odometer, parts and schedule, grouped into your Garage. The car, the truck and the bike each keep their own history.

Can I prove the service history when I sell?

Export the vehicle and you get a ZIP with its full history as a PDF, a CSV and a spreadsheet, with every receipt and photo you attached. Each service record carries the date and the odometer reading it was logged at.
